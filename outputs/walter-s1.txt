Remote work is changing the way organizations think about both productivity and employee wellness. Flexible arrangements for workers have been shown to increase worker job satisfaction and decrease commute-related stress. However, workers do not benefit from flexible working arrangements equally. Many caregivers report having greater difficulty maintaining their focus when performing their job tasks remotely as opposed to in an office setting; early career workers also tend to be missing out on social learning/mentoring opportunities (e.g., "watercooler" conversations) that occur naturally in shared office spaces. Consequently, numerous employers are now developing hybrid models that attempt to offer workers the flexibility of working from home while offering the collaboration potential of in-office work environments. The ultimate success of each model will likely depend upon creating policy around them and communicating clearly.
