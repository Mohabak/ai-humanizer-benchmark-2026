Remote employment has radically changed how companies approach productivity and employee wellness. Research indicates that flexible arrangements improve job satisfaction and reduce stress associated with commuting. But those benefits are not shared equally. People with caring responsibilities often report difficulty concentrating, while those in the early stages of their careers may lose out on informal mentoring, which often takes place in shared offices. Therefore, many organizations are moving toward hybrid models that combine the autonomy of remote employment with the collaboration of an office environment. Ultimately, clear policies and intentional communication will be the key to their long-term success.
